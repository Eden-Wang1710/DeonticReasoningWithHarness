**Case Facts:**
The petitioner filed Form I-526 seeking EB-5 classification and indicated she invested $500,000 on August 30, 2018 in a new commercial enterprise (NCE) associated with a regional center. The NCE’s offering memorandum states it would raise up to $125 million from up to 250 immigrant investors and lend the funds to a job-creating entity (JCE) planning a mixed-use residential and commercial development in California.

The petitioner stated that the investment funds came from a loan of 13,200,000,000 Vietnamese dong (VND) from Military Commercial Joint Stock Bank (MCB), secured by two real properties in Vietnam. She said she entered into a currency-exchange agreement with her former attorney to convert and transfer those VND into U.S. dollars for the EB-5 investment. The record shows that on August 30, 2018 the petitioner withdrew and transferred VND 1,167,000,000 and VND 11,670,000,000 from her account to the Saigon Joint Stock Commercial Bank (SCB) account of the third-party exchanger. On that same day the third party exchanged VND amounts into U.S. dollars (including VND 4,084,500,000 to $175,000; VND 4,317,900,000 to $185,000; VND 1,167,000,000 to $50,000; and VND 3,267,600,000 to $140,000) and transferred $175,000, $185,000, and $140,000 from his SCB account to the NCE’s escrow account at BofI Federal Bank, and $50,000 to the NCE manager’s account.

To show the funds’ provenance, the petitioner submitted an affidavit from the third-party exchanger stating the exchanged funds were the petitioner’s and that he did not use his personal funds for the EB-5 transaction. The record also includes: a statement of funds transfer from the petitioner and the exchanger; the petitioner’s SCB account statement covering August 30, 2018; SCB transaction receipts dated August 30, 2018; the exchanger’s SCB account statement for August 30, 2018; transaction details for the exchanger dated August 29, 2018; remittance application forms dated August 30, 2018; and letters from the NCE president, the escrow agent’s general counsel, and the JCE president.

The petitioner appealed after the adjudicating officer denied the petition on the ground that the adjudicator could not determine that the funds used by the third-party exchanger in the currency exchange were derived from lawful means; the petitioner’s appeal asserts she provided sufficient evidence that the exchanged and transferred U.S. dollars originated from her.
